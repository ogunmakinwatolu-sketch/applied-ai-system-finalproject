# Genre Guide — VibeFinder AI

This document describes the feel, characteristics, and listening context for each
genre in the catalog. It is used as a second retrieval source alongside scored songs
to give Claude richer context when generating recommendations.

---

## pop

Pop music is built for broad appeal — catchy melodies, polished production, and lyrics
that feel relatable to almost anyone. It tends to be upbeat and energetic, with a strong
emphasis on hooks and singalong moments. Pop listeners often want music that matches a
good mood or amplifies one they're trying to get into. It works well as background music
for workouts, commutes, social gatherings, or any moment where you want energy without
a lot of complexity. Pop songs rarely ask you to sit and think — they ask you to feel good.

---

## lofi

Lofi (low fidelity) is defined by its imperfections — soft vinyl crackle, muffled drums,
and melodies that feel like they were recorded in a bedroom at 2am. It is deliberately
understated and repetitive, which makes it ideal for studying, working, or winding down.
The mood is almost always calm, nostalgic, or introspective. Lofi listeners are typically
looking for a sonic environment rather than a song to pay attention to — something that
fills the room without demanding anything from you. It pairs naturally with rain sounds,
late nights, and slow mornings.

---

## rock

Rock is driven by electric guitars, strong rhythms, and a sense of intensity or rebellion.
It covers a wide emotional range — from anthemic and uplifting to aggressive and cathartic.
Rock works well when you need energy with an edge, whether that's a workout, a road trip,
or a moment where you need to push through something difficult. Unlike pop, rock tends to
reward attention — the guitar work, the dynamics, and the lyrics often have more layers to
uncover. Listeners who gravitate toward rock usually want music that feels powerful and real.

---

## ambient

Ambient music prioritizes atmosphere over structure. There are rarely traditional verses or
choruses — instead, sounds layer and evolve slowly, creating a mood or environment rather
than a song. It is the most immersive genre in the catalog, designed to be listened to at
low volume while doing something else, or at high volume with eyes closed. Ambient listeners
are often looking for calm, focus, or a sense of space. It works well for meditation, deep
work, sleep, or any situation where you want music that disappears into the background while
still shaping how you feel.

---

## jazz

Jazz is built on improvisation, complexity, and conversation between instruments. It rewards
careful listening — melodies evolve unexpectedly, rhythms shift, and no two performances are
exactly alike. The mood ranges from relaxed and warm (think coffee shop on a Sunday morning)
to energetic and cerebral. Jazz listeners tend to appreciate musicianship and nuance. It works
well as dinner music, late-night listening, or background for creative work where you want
something engaging but not distracting. If you like jazz, you probably enjoy music that feels
alive and human rather than produced and polished.

---

## synthwave

Synthwave is a nostalgic genre built on 1980s electronic sounds — pulsing synthesizers, driving
drum machines, and cinematic atmospheres. It evokes neon lights, late-night drives, and retrofuturistic
aesthetics. The mood is usually moody, cool, and slightly melancholic even when the tempo is high.
Synthwave listeners are often drawn to the tension between nostalgia and modernity. It works well
for night drives, creative work, gaming, or any situation where you want music that feels like a
movie soundtrack. It shares DNA with ambient in its atmospheric quality but is more rhythmic and energetic.

---

## indie pop

Indie pop sits between pop and alternative — it has pop's melodic sensibility but with more
personality, quirkiness, and emotional honesty. Productions tend to feel warmer and less polished
than mainstream pop, which makes it feel more intimate. The mood is often bittersweet, hopeful,
or reflective. Indie pop listeners usually want music that feels genuine and a little different
from what's on the radio. It works well for quiet afternoons, creative projects, or moments when
you want something upbeat but with more emotional depth than straight pop.
